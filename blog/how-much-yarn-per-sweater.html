How Much Yarn Does a Sweater Need? Consumption, Yarn Count and Costing

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-09

Views: 0

Quick answer

An adult pullover typically consumes somewhere between a few hundred grams and well over a kilogram of yarn — fine-gauge cotton styles at the light end, chunky wool styles at the heavy end. Consumption is never a single fixed figure, because it moves with fabric weight, size, stitch structure, yarn count and wastage. Ask the factory for a per-size estimate built from GSM, the size spec and a stated loss rate.

Yarn is usually the largest material line in a knitwear quotation, so "how much yarn does one sweater need?" is really a costing question. Here is where consumption comes from, how to read yarn counts, and what to ask a supplier.

Why isn't yarn consumption a fixed number?

Two sweaters in the same size can differ in yarn consumption by a factor of three or more. Five variables do most of the work: stitch structure (plain jersey uses the least; ribs, cables and jacquard add yarn, sometimes heavily); fabric weight and gauge, measured in GSM, grams per square metre; size grading, where plus-size ranges add disproportionately; yarn count; and wastage. Our explainer on fabric weight in GSM shows how that weight is measured.

How do you read yarn count — Nm, tex and denier?

Yarn count tells you how thick a yarn is, and it appears in every yarn specification you will receive.

  • Nm (metric count) — the number of 1,000-metre hanks in one kilogram. A higher Nm means a finer yarn: 2/28 Nm is finer than 2/16 Nm. The first figure is the number of plies, so 2/28 Nm is a two-ply yarn.
  • tex — grams per 1,000 metres; a higher tex means a coarser yarn, and tex = 1000 ÷ Nm.
  • denier — grams per 9,000 metres, used mainly for filament and synthetic yarns; denier = 9000 ÷ Nm.

In wool and wool-blend sweaters, chunky woollen-spun yarns generally sit in the 2/8 to 2/16 Nm band, while fine-gauge worsted yarns commonly run from about 2/28 Nm up to 2/48 Nm or finer. Those are industry-typical ranges for orientation, not limits. For the fibres themselves, see our guide to sweater yarns.

How do you estimate yarn consumption from fabric weight?

Start from GSM: grams per square metre multiplied by the garment's surface area gives theoretical yarn weight before losses. Factories run that calculation per size, because panel areas grow at different rates across a graded run. Ask for a table rather than one number — GSM and gauge for each yarn and colourway, consumption per size, the wastage allowance applied on top, and total kilos rounded up to the yarn supplier's sales unit. That is the same arithmetic behind your FOB price; our walkthrough on how to read a sweater quotation explains each line.

How much wastage should you allow?

Knitting always consumes more yarn than the finished garment weighs. Yarn is lost to set-up and trial knitting, broken ends, trimmed edges on fully-fashioned panels, rejected pieces and pre-bulk sampling. Factories typically build in a loss allowance of several percent above theoretical consumption, and structured or heavily cabled styles usually need more than plain ones. Treat any single percentage as a planning assumption rather than a rule.

How does consumption affect sweater yarn cost and lead time?

Yarn is normally the largest single material cost in a basic sweater, so consumption and yarn price multiply into your landed cost. Yarn is spun and dyed by weight and by dye lot, so a small order can oblige you to buy more yarn than the garments need — and that surplus is still paid for. Yarn also adds weeks before knitting starts and is often the longest step in the schedule. That is why small trial orders carry a higher unit cost than repeats: it is the yarn purchase behind them, not the knitting.

Why should you order extra yarn for repeats?

Keep a buffer of the original dye lot if you plan to replenish. A reorder knitted from a new lot can sit visibly beside the first delivery, and seasonal colours or speciality blends are often discontinued, so a substitute changes the hand feel. Agree up front who stores the balance and for how long.

How do you ask a factory for a yarn consumption breakdown?

Send the style reference, size spec, target GSM or gauge and quantity, then ask for four things in writing: the yarn specification (fibre composition, count in Nm or tex, ply and spinning system); consumption per size in grams or kilos per piece; the wastage rate applied and whether sampling yarn is included; and the total yarn to purchase, rounded to the yarn supplier's sales unit. A factory that answers precisely can usually explain its MOQ and sampling plan without hedging. Frequently asked questions

Roughly how many kilos of yarn does a 1,000-piece order need?

Multiply the factory's per-piece figure by 1,000, then add the agreed wastage. If a style's consumption table says 500 g per piece and the loss allowance sits on top, the order lands in the region of several hundred kilos. Always buy against the factory's rounded total rather than your own arithmetic, because yarn is sold by weight and by dye lot.

Does a heavier sweater always mean a bigger yarn bill?

Not necessarily. A chunky 3GG sweater uses more kilos but often a lower-priced woollen-spun yarn, while a light 16GG style may use less weight of a finer, more expensive worsted yarn. Compare the total yarn cost line — kilos multiplied by yarn price — rather than garment weight alone.

Can I send my own yarn to the factory?

Yes, buyer-supplied yarn is common for licensed yarns, speciality fibres or when you already hold stock. You then carry the risk of shortages, dye-lot consistency and delivery timing, and factories often apply a slightly higher loss allowance because they cannot top up locally.
