Cardigan Styles & Knit Jackets: A Buying Guide for Retailers

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-09

Quick answer

A cardigan range usually carries two or three distinct silhouettes rather than five versions of the same button-front shape: button-front classic, zip-through knitted jacket, open-front drape, longline or kimono, and cropped boxy. Because a cardigan is worn open, front and back panels are seen together, so colour and stitch consistency matter more than in a pullover. Inspect front band flatness and equal length after washing, button and zip security, and symmetry between the two front panels.

Cardigans are the layering workhorse of any knitwear range. They extend a brand across seasons, suit almost every customer group, and — unlike pullovers — they get tried on over other clothes, which changes how they must be cut, finished and sized. Here is what a retailer or wholesale buyer should know before ordering them.

The main cardigan silhouettes

  • Button-front classic — the core style. Works in every gauge from chunky 5GG winter knits to fine 14GG merino layers.
  • Zip-through knitted jacket — reads more sporty and technical; zips add cost but raise perceived value.
  • Open-front or drape cardigan — no fastening, softer drape, popular in womenswear and maternity-friendly fits.
  • Longline and kimono styles — fall below the hip; strong on hangers and in online photography.
  • Cropped boxy cardigans — a trend-led silhouette aimed at younger customers.

A balanced range usually carries two or three of these silhouettes rather than five variations of the same button-front shape.

Yarn and stitch decisions

Because a cardigan is worn open, the back and front panels are visible together — so colour and stitch consistency matter more than in a pullover. Chunky cable cardigans in wool blends suit autumn capsules; fine rib cardigans in viscose or cotton blends carry spring. Brushed and bouclé yarns add texture that photographs well for online stores.

Bands, buttons and zips are where cardigans differ most from pullovers in production. Buttonhole quality, placket stability and zip tape flatness are the details that separate a premium cardigan from a cheap one — inspect all three on every sample.

Who buys which style

  • Womenswear retailers — drape, longline and cropped styles, fine gauges, soft blends.
  • Menswear and unisex labels — shawl collars, zip knitted jackets, heavier gauges.
  • Kids' lines — button-front classics in machine-washable blends.
  • Older customers — easier fastenings, roomier fits, mid-weight warmth.

Ordering cardigans: what to confirm with your supplier

Because construction is more complex than a pullover, put these points in writing early: placket and band construction, button or zip sourcing, lining (if any), and whether pockets are welt, patch or none. Confirm sample costs per style — cardigan samples cost more than pullover samples because of the trims and hand work.

Quality checks specific to cardigans

  • Front bands lie flat and are the same length after washing.
  • Buttons and zips are securely attached with reinforced stitching.
  • Symmetry: both front panels match in stripe, texture and length.
  • Collar and cuff recovery after stretch.
