Dead Stock and Overstock Knitwear: How Brands Clear It Without Damaging the Brand

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-09

Views: 0

Quick answer

Clearing overstock knitwear is not simply “put it on sale”: routes differ sharply in how much cash they recover, how fast they empty the warehouse and how much they damage full-price perception. Too shallow a discount leaves the stock sitting; too deep a cut teaches customers to wait for markdowns. The most reliable fix is upstream — buying a smaller first order and replenishing proven styles — rather than clearing a large mistake at the end of the season.

Every knitwear season ends with some stock left over, and how a brand clears it is a pricing decision as much as a logistics one. Dead stock handled clumsily trains customers to wait for discounts, drags down next season's margins and ties up cash that should fund the next buy.

Why does dead stock accumulate?

Overstock rarely has one cause. The usual mix:

  • An over-optimistic first order — buying to a hoped-for sell-through rather than a tested one.
  • Too many styles and colourways, spreading a fixed sales total thinly across the range.
  • Colour or fit out of step with the market — a shade that photographed well but sold poorly.
  • A short season — unseasonable weather can remove weeks from the selling window.
  • Competitor discounting that pulls full-price demand away late in the season.

Which clearance route should you choose?

Each route trades cash recovery against speed and brand perception. Match the route to the style, not to the size of the pile.

  • Early mid-season markdown. The best recovery and the least brand damage, because the stock is still in season and the discount is small. It depends on spotting slow sellers early — review sell-through by style, colour and size at mid-season, not at the end.
  • End-of-season sale. The standard route: moderate recovery, fast clearance, and the deepest risk to brand positioning if discounts become predictable. Keep markdowns inside the structure you planned for.
  • Jobbing to an off-price buyer. Selling the balance as a single lot clears the warehouse quickly, but the unit price is far below retail and the goods may reappear in discount channels where you do not control presentation.
  • Staff sales. Decent recovery, confidential and popular internally, provided you set purchase limits so the stock is not resold.
  • Donation. No cash recovery, but a genuine CSR outcome and possible tax treatment depending on your market; some destinations have specific rules on what may be donated and how it must be documented.
  • Resale or consignment platforms. Recovery is unpredictable and you must absorb photography, listing and shipping work — reasonable for premium pieces, inefficient for basics.
  • Carry over to next season. Works for basics and neutral colours that do not date. A fashion shade or season-specific silhouette usually sells worse in its second year, and holding it costs storage the whole time.

How do you combine clearance routes?

Most brands use several routes at once, assigned by style role: carry basics and neutral colours over, mark down trend colours early while the season can still sell them, and clear genuine image pieces through staff sales or off-price rather than a public discount on the main line. The more visible the discount, the more it should be reserved for stock you cannot sell any other way.

How do you reduce overstock before it happens?

The cheapest clearance is the stock you never bought.

  • Buy the first order as a test, not a bet. Order enough to learn the sell-through rate, and keep the depth for confirmed winners — a lower first order is far cheaper than end-of-season markdowns. Our guide to sweater MOQ explains how minimums shape that decision.
  • Limit style and colour count. Fewer, better-chosen options concentrate sales and make each likelier to sell through — the logic covered in knitwear range planning.
  • Replace depth with replenishment. A proven style can be repeated; a large initial commitment cannot be undone. Agree repeat lead times before the season starts.
  • Weight the buy toward neutrals and buy fashion shades shallow, since a neutral that misses still sells next year.
  • Include a markdown allowance in your pricing so clearing stock does not wipe out the season's margin.

What does overstock actually cost?

Unsold knitwear is bulky and slow to clear, so the cost is not only the margin you give up. Cash sits in boxes instead of funding the next buy; warehousing and insurance continue; and the markdown that finally moves the goods reduces the margin on the whole collection, including the styles that sold well. Set against that, the cost of ordering a little less is usually small.

What should you watch when clearing stock?

  • Compliance does not lapse. Discounted goods still need correct fibre content, care labels and market compliance — a clearance channel is not an exemption.
  • Protect the main channel. Price clearance stock far enough from current-season prices, and use channels that do not compete with your own store or marketplace listings.
  • Avoid a permanent discount habit. If customers learn that everything is reduced by a set point in the season, they stop buying at full price — the most expensive outcome of all.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to discount early or wait until the end of the season?

Early, for anything already showing weak sell-through. A small mid-season reduction still sells at a time when customers want the product; waiting until the season is over typically forces a deeper cut and leaves less time to clear the balance. The condition is identifying slow sellers early, which requires reviewing sales by style, colour and size at mid-season.

Can overstock knitwear be carried over to next season?

Only if it is a basic style in a neutral colour that does not date. Fashion shades, novelty textures and season-specific silhouettes usually sell worse in their second year, and keeping them costs storage in the meantime. For those, an off-price or staff route recovers cash sooner.

How can a small brand avoid dead stock on a first collection?

Keep the first order shallow, limit the number of styles and colourways, and buy neutrals more deeply than fashion shades. Then use repeat production to add depth to whatever proves itself. Choosing a manufacturer with low minimums and a workable repeat lead time is what makes that strategy possible.
