Knitwear Shipping Documents: The Set That Gets Cargo

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-10

Quick answer

A sweater shipment needs four documents to move and one to be released: a commercial invoice, a packing list, a bill of lading, and a certificate of origin or declaration, plus a telex release or original B/L to collect the goods. The invoice and packing list are produced by the supplier, the B/L by the carrier, and the origin document by whoever the destination scheme requires. Almost every cargo delay at destination is one of these being wrong, late or inconsistent with the others.

Buyers focus on price, quality and delivery date, then discover at the port that the cartons cannot be released because the invoice says 60 percent cotton and the care label says 55. Documentation is the least interesting part of an import and the one most likely to cost a week, and it is entirely preventable.

Which documents does a sweater shipment actually need?

Four in the core set, plus one release mechanism.

  • Commercial invoice. The customs value declaration: what the goods are, how many, the unit price, the total, the terms of sale and the origin. It is the document duty is calculated from, so every figure on it matters.
  • Packing list. The physical inventory: how many cartons, what is in each, the size and colour assortment, net and gross weight, and the carton dimensions. This is what the receiving warehouse counts against.
  • Bill of lading (B/L). Issued by the carrier. It is a receipt for the goods, the contract of carriage, and — in negotiable form — a document of title, which is why it controls who can collect the cargo.
  • Certificate of origin, or an origin declaration. Proves where the goods were made. Required for preferential duty claims, and requested by some markets regardless. Our notes on reducing import duty explain why origin is a separate question from where the goods shipped from.
  • Telex release or the original B/L. The mechanism that lets the consignee take delivery. A telex release is the carrier confirming the original has been surrendered at origin, so the consignee can collect against a copy.

Beyond the core set, a shipment may need an inspection certificate, a test report for restricted substances, a fumigation or ISPM 15 declaration for wooden packaging, and insurance documentation. Our notes on pre-shipment inspection cover where the inspection report fits.

Why does the telex release cause so many delays?

Because it is the one document that depends on someone at origin doing something after the vessel has sailed. The carrier will not release cargo without either the original B/L in hand at destination or a telex release confirming the originals were surrendered.

Three things go wrong. The supplier holds the original B/L until the balance payment clears, which is a legitimate commercial protection but means the release only happens once the money arrives — so a payment delayed by a week is a cargo delay of a week. The bank handling a letter of credit may hold documents for its own checks. Or the surrender instruction is simply sent late, because nobody owned the task after dispatch.

The practical control is to agree in the order who surrenders the B/L, when, and against what condition. Our notes on payment terms explain the commercial logic behind holding documents, and supply contracts cover where the obligation belongs.

What makes documents get rejected?

Not usually a wrong document — usually an inconsistent one. The common failures:

Fibre percentages that do not match. The invoice, the packing list description, the care label and the test report must all state the same composition. A mismatch is the most common reason a shipment is held, and it is a compliance issue rather than a clerical one because the label is a legal declaration.

Quantities that do not reconcile. The invoice says 1,200 pieces, the packing list totals 1,180 because ten were short-shipped and twenty were added from another lot. Short shipments happen and are legitimate, but they have to be documented rather than left for the receiver to discover.

Description that does not match the HS code. A "knitted top" classified under a sweater heading invites the broker to query it. Our notes on HS codes explain how the classification is decided.

Who produces each document?

The supplier produces the commercial invoice, the packing list, and usually the origin declaration. These are the ones a buyer can influence directly, by specifying what must appear on them.

The carrier or freight forwarder issues the B/L and handles the telex release. Our notes on sea freight cover the booking and loading side.

The buyer or importer of record handles the customs entry itself, including duty and VAT, and may need to provide an importer number or a power of attorney to a broker.

Third parties — a testing lab, an inspection firm, a chamber of commerce for origin certification — produce their own certificates, and those have lead times that need booking.

What does a letter of credit change?

If payment runs through an L/C, the document set stops being an administrative file and becomes the instrument of payment. The bank checks every document against the credit terms line by line, and any difference — a port name spelled differently, a missing signature, a description that does not match the credit word for word — is a discrepancy that can let the bank refuse.

What makes this brutal is that the goods may already be on the water when the discrepancy is found. The practical controls are to have the supplier check its document templates against the credit before production, to require draft documents for review, and to keep the credit's own wording as the master text for the invoice description rather than the purchase order's. Our notes on payment terms explain where an L/C sits against simpler arrangements.

How should a buyer specify the document set?

  • List the required documents in the purchase order, naming each one and who provides it. "All standard export documents" is not a specification.
  • Fix the composition string once and reuse it. One exact text — "55% cotton, 45% polyester" — used identically on the invoice, packing list and label. Most mismatches come from the same fact being written three different ways.
  • Agree the release mechanism up front. Telex release or original B/L, and under what payment condition. Our notes on payment terms cover the trade-off.
  • Ask for draft documents before the vessel sails. A draft invoice and packing list checked two days before departure is cheap; discovering the error at destination is not.
  • Check origin requirements for the destination, not just the duty rate. Our notes on the UK market and Australia and New Zealand cover two markets where origin and biosecurity paperwork catch people out.
  • Keep the whole set with the order file. A dispute about what was shipped is settled by documents, and reconstructing them months later is far harder than filing them.

Frequently asked questions

What is a telex release and why does it matter?

It is the carrier confirming that the original bill of lading has been surrendered at origin, so the consignee can collect the cargo against a copy instead of waiting for paper to arrive by courier. It matters because cargo cannot be released without either the original B/L or a telex release, so a delay in issuing one becomes a delay in collecting the goods — often the point at which demurrage starts.

Who prepares the commercial invoice and packing list?

The supplier prepares both. The commercial invoice declares the customs value, quantity, terms and origin; the packing list gives the physical breakdown by carton, size, colour and weight. The buyer's leverage is in specifying exactly what must appear on them, particularly the composition string and the quantity reconciliation.

Why do documents get held at customs?

Most often because they contradict each other rather than because one is missing. Fibre percentages that differ between invoice, label and test report, quantities that do not reconcile after a short shipment, or a description that does not match the declared HS code are the recurring causes. Fixing the composition string once and reusing it identically removes the largest single source of holds.
