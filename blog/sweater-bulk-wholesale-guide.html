Sweater Wholesale & Bulk Orders: How to Plan Your First Order

Views: 0

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-09

Placing your first bulk sweater order can feel daunting. You have a broad idea of what you want to sell, but there are a lot of moving parts: yarn, gauge, sizes, colors, MOQ, sampling, deposits, production time, shipping and customs. The buyers who succeed treat it like a project with stages, not a single transaction.

Here is a straightforward path from your first idea to delivered cartons, plus a checklist you can reuse for every order.

1. Lock down your market and styles

Before you talk to a factory, decide what you are actually selling and to whom. A fashion brand in a cold climate will want cozy wool and cashmere blends; a summer e-commerce line needs lightweight cotton or viscose knits. Your market drives the yarn, the silhouette, the color palette and how many sizes you carry.

Start with a shortlist of 2–4 styles you want to test. Trying to launch a full collection on your first order spreads your budget thin and makes sampling and production harder to manage. A focused first run lets you validate demand before committing to a larger range.

2. Choose your yarn and construction

The yarn is the single biggest factor in how your sweater looks, feels, performs and costs. Talk to your supplier about the trade-offs:

  • Wool and wool blends — warm, breathable, premium feel; a strong choice for autumn and winter.
  • Cashmere and blended cashmere — soft and luxurious, with a higher price point.
  • Acrylic and polyester blends — affordable, easy-care and durable; popular for everyday basics.
  • Cotton and viscose — cooler and lighter; ideal for transitional and summer knits.

Gauge matters too. A finer gauge gives a cleaner, dressier garment; a chunkier gauge gives a cozy, textured look. If you're unsure, ask the factory to suggest a yarn and gauge that fits your target price and season — an experienced manufacturer will have clear opinions.

3. Understand MOQ and how to negotiate it

Minimum order quantity is one of the first questions buyers ask. MOQ varies by style, yarn and gauge, and it isn't set in stone. A few ways to make a smaller first order work:

  • Ask for the MOQ per style, not just for the whole order.
  • Ask if you can split sizes or colors within a single style to reach the minimum.
  • Ask about a smaller "trial order" or a sample run — many suppliers, including YZR Sweaters, are flexible for a first batch.
  • Confirm whether sample fees are credited back to your bulk order.

A lower MOQ usually means a slightly higher unit price because production and setup are spread over fewer pieces. That's normal — the point of flexible MOQ is to let you de-risk before scaling.

4. Sample before you commit

Never skip the sample. This is where you confirm that the fit, gauge, stitch, color and hand-feel match what you imagined. Give the factory a clear tech pack or, if you're starting from a reference photo, be specific about the details you care about. Expect at least one round of sampling and possibly a second round of minor revisions before you sign off.

When you approve the sample, keep a reference copy. It becomes the benchmark your bulk order is measured against.

5. Understand what goes into the price

Several factors determine the unit cost, and knowing them helps you compare quotes intelligently rather than just chasing the lowest number:

  • Yarn type, weight and count
  • Gauge and stitch complexity (plain, jacquard, cable knit)
  • Garment size and the number of sizes
  • Your total quantity and how the costs are spread
  • Trims — buttons, zips, drawcords, woven labels, hang tags
  • Finishing and packaging requirements

Ask your supplier to itemize the quote so you can see where the value is. If a quote seems far below everyone else, ask how they intend to achieve it — that question alone often reveals a lot.

6. Bulk production and quality control

Once the sample is approved, production begins. Ask about their QC process: are there in-process checks during knitting and assembly, and a final inspection before packing? You can also arrange an independent third-party inspection before shipment. Agree in writing on the acceptance criteria and the tolerance for things like shading and gauge so there's no disagreement at the end.

7. Packaging, lead times and shipping

Plan your packaging early. Decide on polybags, hangers, tissue, boxes, and any private-label touches like woven neck labels, care labels and hang tags. Bundling your label and packaging with production is far more efficient than retrofitting it later.

Get a written schedule for both sampling and bulk, and work backwards from your launch date to set the deposit and delivery terms. For international orders, confirm your incoterms (such as FOB or DDP), who handles customs clearance, and whether the factory supports the export documentation your shipping agent needs.

Your bulk order checklist

  • Market, season and target customer defined
  • 2–4 styles shortlisted
  • Yarn, gauge and construction agreed
  • MOQ and any trial-order options confirmed in writing
  • Sample developed, reviewed and approved (with reference copy kept)
  • Quote itemized and understood
  • Deposit, payment terms and delivery terms agreed
  • QC plan and inspection criteria set
  • Packaging, labels and trims planned
  • Lead time and shipping/customs arrangements confirmed
