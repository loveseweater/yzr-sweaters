Knit Vests and Co-ord Sets: A Growing Knitwear Category

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-09

Views: 0

Two knitwear categories have quietly grown from niche to essential: the knit vest and the co-ord set. Both solve the same problem for modern customers — how to look put-together in mid-season weather when a heavy sweater is too much but a t-shirt is too little. For buyers, they also open up higher-margin, lower-weight orders that ship efficiently.

Why the knit vest works

The knit vest is one of the most versatile pieces in a knitwear range. It layers over shirts and blouses, works alone in warmer months, and photographs well because it holds its shape. Unlike heavier knitwear, it uses less yarn, which means a lower unit cost and a more accessible price point for customers.

  • Layering piece — extends wear into spring and autumn.
  • Lower yarn usage — favourable cost per unit.
  • Forgiving fit — less size-sensitive than a fitted sweater.
  • Trend-friendly — easy to update with colour and stitch.

The classic V-neck vest with ribbed trims is the safest bet for a broad market. Cropped and boxy versions suit younger, trend-led customers.

Co-ord sets: selling two pieces at once

A knitted co-ord set — typically a matching top and skirt, or a vest and wide-leg pant — is attractive to both the customer and the retailer. The customer gets a complete outfit with minimal decision-making; the retailer gets a higher transaction value and a styled look that merchandises itself in store and online.

Co-ords also photograph unusually well for e-commerce, because a two-piece set gives the impression of a considered outfit rather than a single garment.

Yarn and stitch choices

  • Fine-gauge cotton or viscose blends — best for spring/summer vests and sets.
  • Merino and wool blends — move the same silhouettes into autumn.
  • Rib structures — hold shape well and read as premium in a tank or vest.
  • Pointelle and open stitches — add texture without weight.

Because vests and sets use less yarn than a chunky sweater, the fibre choice has a smaller effect on total cost — which means buyers can often afford a nicer yarn than they expect.

Sizing and fit considerations

Vests are generally less size-sensitive than sweaters, which reduces return rates. Co-ords are more sizing-critical because both pieces must fit, though generous, elasticated waists make this easier. Always confirm the top and bottom are graded consistently — a set where the skirt runs small against the top will generate returns.

How to add them to a range

  • Start with one safe V-neck vest in two or three core colours.
  • Add one co-ord set as a statement piece for your hero photography.
  • Keep colour palettes consistent so vests styled over your existing tops.
  • Order in a smaller quantity first and reorder winners — these categories move fast.
