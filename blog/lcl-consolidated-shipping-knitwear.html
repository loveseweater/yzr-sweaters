LCL Shipping for Knitwear: When Consolidation Saves Money (and When It Doesn't)

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-10

Quick answer

LCL (less-than-container-load) makes sense for knitwear shipments under roughly 12–15 cubic metres — below that, paying per cubic metre beats renting a whole 20ft box. Above it, FCL is usually cheaper and always gentler on the goods. The gotchas: destination CFS charges that dwarf the ocean freight, extra handling that raises damage and moisture risk for knitwear, and slower transit because your cartons wait for other people's cargo. Protect accordingly and read the all-in quote, not the headline rate.

A buyer shipping 800 sweaters asks us for "sea freight, cheapest way" at least once a week, and the honest answer is usually LCL — but with warnings attached. Consolidated shipping is genuinely cost-effective at small volumes, yet the quotes buyers compare are rarely comparable, and knitwear is more vulnerable to LCL's rough edges than most cargo. Here's how we think about it when we book shipments for our own customers.

The real break-even point

The naive comparison is per-CBM rate versus container rate, and that comparison lies. A realistic one includes both ends:

  • Under ~10 CBM: LCL almost always wins, even after destination charges. A 20ft FCL at 28 CBM capacity is mostly air at this volume.
  • 10–15 CBM: the grey zone. Get both quotes with all-in destination charges (CFS handling, documentation, delivery order fees) before deciding. Destination CFS fees in the US and EU routinely run USD 150–400 per shipment regardless of size, and they erode the LCL advantage fast.
  • Over ~15 CBM: take the 20ft container. You get a sealed box, one handling event, and a simpler claim if anything goes wrong. Our container guide covers FCL loading in detail.

For reference, 800–1,000 folded, polybagged sweaters in export cartons is typically 4–6 CBM — comfortably LCL territory. A 5,000-piece order at 25–30 CBM is a container.

What consolidation does to knitwear specifically

Knitwear is soft, absorbent and crease-prone, and LCL exposes it to things FCL doesn't:

  • Double handling. Your cartons are unloaded into a container freight station (CFS), consolidated, deconsolidated at destination and delivered. Every touch is a chance for a crushed carton or a wet floor. Specify five-ply export cartons and, if the season is humid, a desiccant pack per carton — mould in transit is a real claim we see, and our mould prevention guide applies doubly to LCL.
  • Neighbours. Your sweaters share a box with whatever else consolidated that week. We've seen knitwear arrive smelling of the chemical drums it travelled beside. Reputable forwarders segregate incompatible cargo; cheap ones discover the concept after your claim.
  • Time. Consolidation adds roughly 5–10 days at origin and deconsolidation a few more at destination. If your launch date is fixed, that buffer matters more than the freight saving — compare honestly against air freight for the urgent portion.

Reading an LCL quote without getting stung

The headline "USD 35/CBM" is ocean freight only. Ask for the full picture before comparing:

  • Origin charges: pickup, CFS receiving, documentation, export customs.
  • Destination charges: CFS handling, D/O fee, customs clearance, delivery to your warehouse. This is where the surprises live — get them in writing.
  • Minimum billing: most LCL tariffs have a 1 CBM minimum, and some routes bill 2. Small sample-ish shipments can be cheaper by courier — see sample shipping by courier.
  • Insurance: LCL's extra handling is exactly why cargo insurance is worth its 0.3–0.5% here. Check the policy covers consolidated loads specifically.

Incoterms matter too: on EXW you own all of the above; on FOB the supplier handles origin side. If you're new to this, our Incoterms guide for knitwear will save you a misunderstanding or three.

When we steer buyers away from LCL

Two situations. First, when the volume is within striking distance of a container — the incremental cost of a 20ft box over 14 CBM of LCL is often under USD 500 all-in, and the damage risk drops to near zero. Second, when the goods are premium: a cashmere blend at USD 40 a piece deserves a sealed container or air freight, not a shared box with someone's tile samples.

Frequently asked questions

How many sweaters fit in one cubic metre?

Folded and polybagged in standard export cartons, roughly 150–200 adult sweaters per CBM depending on fabric weight and size range. Chunky 5gg cardigans run lower, fine-gauge tops higher. Your factory should give you the carton dimensions and total CBM with the packing list — ask before you book freight, not after.

Is LCL insured differently from FCL?

The insurance product is the same, but the risk profile isn't: consolidated cargo sees more handling events, and insurers know it. Confirm your policy has no exclusion for consolidated shipments, and photograph cartons at the factory gate — it makes claims dramatically easier.

Why is my LCL quote so cheap but the arrival invoice so big?

Because ocean freight is the small part. Destination CFS handling, delivery order fees, customs clearance and local delivery routinely total several hundred dollars per shipment and are quoted separately — or not quoted at all. Always ask forwarders for the door-to-door, all-in figure before you compare options.
