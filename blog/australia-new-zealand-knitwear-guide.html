Selling Knitwear in Australia and New Zealand: Seasons, Sizing and Rules

By YZR Sweaters · 2026-09

Quick answer

Australia and New Zealand are two separate markets and two separate customs territories, both buying on a reverse season — winter runs June to August, so orders are placed from about November to February. Australian and New Zealand sizing follows its own charts rather than US ones. Both countries enforce strict biosecurity rules that affect packaging and clean-goods declarations, and both generally apply low or zero duty on apparel while collecting GST.

Most sourcing advice is written for buyers in the northern hemisphere, which makes the ANZ market look like an afterthought: a small population, a long way away, buying at the wrong time of year. In practice it suits a supplier well. Australian and New Zealand winters arrive when Chinese factories are quiet, the freight lane from South China is short, and the market pays for quality knitwear because consumers need warmth for part of the year and layering for the rest of it.

Why does the reverse season suit a knitwear supplier?

Australian winter runs roughly June to August, with the colder southern states — Victoria, Tasmania, the ACT and parts of New South Wales — starting earlier and running longer. New Zealand runs slightly later, typically June to September, with real cold in the South Island. That means ANZ orders land in the production window that northern-hemisphere buyers have already vacated.

The buying calendar follows the season rather than the calendar year. Autumn/winter ranges are committed from about November through February and delivered from April through June, with the balance arriving before the winter markdown period in July. Our notes on reverse-season buying cover the timing logic, and the knitwear buying calendar shows where ANZ sits against northern-hemisphere programmes.

One consequence catches people out: Christmas is summer in Australia and New Zealand. Knitwear is not the Christmas gifting category there in the way it is in Europe or North America. The gift season in ANZ sits around June, coinciding with winter rather than December.

What does the ANZ climate mean for the product mix?

The climate is milder than the latitude suggests. Coastal Sydney, Melbourne, Perth, Brisbane and Auckland see cool rather than severe winters, which favours fine and mid-gauge knitwear over heavy chunky pieces. Inland and upland areas — Canberra, the Victorian high country, Tasmania, New Zealand's South Island — do get genuine cold, and there the heavier gauges work.

A practical mix for a first ANZ range: fine-gauge merino and merino-blend sweaters for the shoulder seasons and the coastal cities; mid-weight lambswool crew necks and cardigans as the core winter style; a small selection of heavier knits for the colder inland regions; and cotton and cotton-blend knits for the warm months, which in northern Australia and much of Queensland run most of the year. Our notes on transitional season knitwear describe the mid-weight logic that suits this market well, and our cotton sourcing guide covers the warm-weather end of the range.

Wool is well regarded in ANZ, and Australian consumers have a domestic familiarity with merino that works in your favour — the fibre reads as local and trustworthy even when the garment is made offshore. Colour runs relatively conservative: navy, charcoal, oatmeal, cream and camel carry the core.

What sizing does the Australian and New Zealand market use?

ANZ womenswear uses its own numbering, broadly aligned with UK numbering but not interchangeable with it, and definitely not interchangeable with US sizing. A US size 8 is not an Australian size 8, and relabelling a US chart is the mistake that shows up as returns. Men's knitwear is frequently sold by chest measurement in centimetres, and the fit expectation is relaxed rather than tailored.

Two fit tendencies are worth knowing. ANZ consumers on average prefer a slightly longer body length than the European norm and a more relaxed shoulder than the Japanese or German market expects. Plus sizes are a meaningful part of demand, and a range that skips them leaves volume on the table.

Build the chart from ANZ body measurements rather than converting from another base, and measure the approved sample against it. Our notes on sizing and grading set out how to build that chart, and measuring a sweater covers how each point is taken.

What labelling and biosecurity rules apply in Australia and New Zealand?

Labelling. Australia requires fibre content to be disclosed and enforced by the ACCC, with country of origin also required. New Zealand requires fibre content under its Fair Trading Act. Care labelling follows the AS/NZS care labelling standard, close to the international symbol system but with its own variants.

Biosecurity is the requirement most suppliers have never encountered, and it is not optional. Australia and New Zealand operate some of the strictest biosecurity regimes in the world, and natural-fibre goods can attract inspection if packaging carries soil, plant matter or insect activity. Keep cartons clean and dry, avoid unprocessed wooden packaging unless it carries ISPM 15 treatment marking, and declare the goods accurately. A shipment held for inspection is a delay worth planning around, not a compliance failure on your part. Chemical compliance runs through each country's own scheme, and most large ANZ retailers will ask for Oeko-Tex or equivalent test reports.

How do duty, GST and freight work into ANZ?

Apparel duty into Australia and New Zealand is generally low, and many knitwear lines enter at zero or a nominal rate under the countries' MFN schedules. Both countries apply GST rather than VAT — 10 percent in Australia, 15 percent in New Zealand — and commercial consignments should assume standard treatment rather than relying on low-value thresholds. Your buyer's customs broker is the right place to confirm current rates.

Australia and New Zealand are separate customs territories, so a single shipment cannot serve both as one customs entry. Plan two entries if the buyer sells in both. Note also that Chinese-origin goods do not gain preferential treatment by transiting through Australia — the origin rule is the subject of our notes on reducing import duty.

Freight is the pleasant part. Transit from South China to Sydney, Melbourne or Auckland is commonly around eighteen to twenty-five days port to port, materially shorter than to Europe or the US East Coast, and there is no pre-Christmas congestion peak. Our notes on sea freight for sweaters cover container and packing decisions.

How should you approach an ANZ buyer?

  • Lead with the season match. An ANZ buyer's biggest structural problem is finding a supplier with capacity in their delivery window. Say plainly that you can produce for a June delivery without competing with a northern-hemisphere peak.
  • Send an ANZ size chart with the sample, not a US or European one, with a relaxed fit for the core and a closer fit for fashion.
  • Offer a mid-weight-weighted range with a cotton component. Heavy chunky knits should be a minor part of the range, not the centre of it.
  • Confirm biosecurity readiness. Say that cartons are clean, that wooden packaging carries ISPM 15 marking, and that you are content for the goods to be inspected on arrival.
  • Keep freight terms simple. Most ANZ buyers work FOB with their own forwarder or a delivered arrangement; our Incoterms guide explains where risk and cost transfer.

Frequently asked questions

Is Australian sizing the same as UK sizing?

Broadly similar in numbering for womenswear, but not interchangeable, and neither is interchangeable with US sizing. The safe approach is to build the chart from Australian body measurements and re-measure the approved sample against it, rather than assuming a chart from another market will transfer.

Do I need special packaging to ship knitwear to Australia or New Zealand?

No specialist packaging, but both countries enforce strict biosecurity rules. Keep cartons clean and free of soil and plant matter, make sure any wooden packaging carries ISPM 15 treatment marking, and declare the contents accurately. Goods may be inspected on arrival, so allow for a short delay in the delivery plan.

Is duty high on knitwear imported into Australia or New Zealand?

Generally not. Apparel tariffs into both countries are low, with many knitwear lines entering at zero or a nominal rate. Both apply GST instead of VAT, at 10 percent in Australia and 15 percent in New Zealand, and current thresholds and procedures are best confirmed with your buyer's customs broker.
